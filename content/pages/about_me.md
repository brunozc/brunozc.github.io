I study how soils behave under dynamic and cyclic loading, and how that behaviour governs the performance of the
infrastructure built on them. Most of my work is on railways: the vibrations generated by passing trains and their
propagation as waves through the ground, the critical speed of trains on soft soils, and the accumulation of
permanent deformation in the track and in the soil underneath it. I apply the same approaches to other geotechnical
infrastructure, such as embankments, flood defences and pipelines.

This interest goes back to my PhD at Delft University of Technology on the dynamics of railway transition zones in
soft soils. Since then I have combined field measurements with numerical models, from the finite element method to
the material point method, and with constitutive models for the cyclic behaviour of soft soils.

I am equally interested in what data and machine learning can add to these models. I develop machine-learning and
data-fusion methods to characterise the subsurface from sparse in-situ tests and geophysical surveys, and hybrid
models that update numerical simulations as monitoring data become available. I believe the future of engineering modelling lies in
such hybrid models, which learn from data while remaining grounded in physics.

I also care about making research usable, so most of my models are released as
[open-source software]({{< relref "software.md" >}}), such as STEM for railway-induced vibrations, ROSE for railway
track degradation and SchemaGAN for subsurface schematisation.

## Experience

{{< entry period="2013 – present" title="Deltares" url="https://www.deltares.nl/en" subtitle="Expert in geotechnical dynamics and railway infrastructure" >}}
I coordinate national and international research projects on geotechnical railway engineering and on the
application of machine learning in geotechnical engineering, and I supervise MSc students on these topics. From 2022
to 2025, I was a member of the [Young Scientific Council of Deltares](https://www.deltares.nl/en/news/meet-the-scientific-council-of-deltares/),
which acts as the scientific conscience of Deltares and represents its scientific expertise.

My projects address the prediction of railway-induced vibrations
and of critical train speeds on soft soils,
railway track degradation and
the performance of transition zones, the
stability of railway embankments, and
the data fusion of open datasets
into three-dimensional subsurface schematisations. I also develop machine-learning methods for subsurface
characterisation, such as generative adversarial networks and deep reinforcement learning.

More about my work is on my [Deltares profile](https://www.deltares.nl/en/expertise/our-people/bruno-zuada-coelho).
{{< /entry >}}

{{< entry period="2016" title="Chalmers University of Technology" url="https://www.chalmers.se/en/" subtitle="Visiting Researcher · January – April" >}}
Developed a novel constitutive model for the permanent deformation of soft soils under cyclic loading. The model
accounts for fundamental features of natural soft soil behaviour, such as anisotropy, bonding and rate dependence,
and describes the accumulation of strain through a viscoplastic multiplier.
{{< /entry >}}

{{< entry period="2015" title="University of Cambridge" url="https://www.cam.ac.uk/" subtitle="Marie Curie Research Fellow · September – December" >}}
Modelled large-deformation problems in geomechanics with the Material Point Method. This included the development
and validation of the double-point formulation of the Material Point Method, in which the solid and liquid phases
are modelled separately.
{{< /entry >}}

{{< entry period="2011 – 2013" title="TNO" url="https://www.tno.nl/en/" subtitle="Research Scientist" >}}
Worked on the European project [CargoVibes](https://cordis.europa.eu/project/id/266248/reporting), which
developed a new track system (ladder track) to reduce the vibrations generated by freight trains. This included
field measurement campaigns in Poland, Belgium and the Netherlands, and numerical tools to evaluate how track
degradation affects the generation of vibrations. Also worked on experimental and numerical studies of
railway-induced vibrations and their effects on nearby dwellings, and developed software for real-time data
acquisition and processing.
{{< /entry >}}
